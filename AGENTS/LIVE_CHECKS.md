# Live checks

Verification that only a real call, the human's own Chrome, or a real file on disk can provide. Each entry has a **Trigger**, exact **Steps**, **Success criteria** and **On failure**. Run entries whose trigger has fired; remove them when they pass; fix forward when they fail.

Agents queue entries here when their PR's behaviour cannot be exercised by the e2e harness (fake devices, no Meet). This is the project's counterpart to a post-deploy verification list.

---

### The transcription steps on a real call in Brave

**Trigger**: progress as steps (#31) merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`; the next real call transcribed, ten minutes or longer, watched from Save to Done.
**Steps**: after Save in the stop window, keep the processing tab that opens in view and open the Rescribe tab next to it. Watch the row and the tab through the run. Note the step count shown at the start and at the end, the order of the running steps, the `at m:ss of m:ss` position on each channel step, the `about N min left on this step` on the first channel and `about N min left` on the last, once each appears, and the time each actually took from then.
**Success criteria**: the same numbered steps in the row, the tab and the Record line, in this order: Speech model (only when the model had to be loaded), `<my name>’s microphone`, The call, Who said what, Transcript files; the count never changes during the run; the position on a channel step climbs through the recording and the segment fills once, then the next step takes over (it must be obvious that the call is a second step, not the microphone again); each time left is within a factor of two of the truth once it shows; nowhere the words `mic`, `display` or `regions`. The e2e walk (`e2e/progress.spec.ts`) checks all of this on a 40 s recording; only a long real call shows whether the pace and the time left hold up over minutes.
**On failure**: a step count that changes, a segment that fills twice, or a time left far off: file it with the screenshots (the row and the tab at the moment) and the lengths of the two channels' speech (the `transcript.json` segments per channel).

### WebGPU transcription runs on the GPU in Brave

**Trigger**: fired by part 2 of #2: the backend setting exists in Settings ("Speech engine backend").
**Steps**: Settings → backend WebGPU; Re-run transcription on the 58-minute call of 2026-09-25 11:30 (WASM took 119 minutes for it, 28 of them starved by another process; both channels must be transcribed again, so keep the media) with the app tab's DevTools console open (the worker's messages show there). Look for `[Parakeet.js] Creating ONNX sessions with execution mode 'webgpu-hybrid'` and for any ORT warning that says it is _removing_ the `webgpu` execution provider. Note the transcription time, then repeat with WASM on the same recording.
**Success criteria**: a transcript, no fallback warning, WebGPU faster than WASM. Headless Chromium has WebGPU (ENVIRONMENT_NOTES) and `e2e/transcribe.spec.ts` covers it with `RESCRIBE_E2E_WEBGPU=1`; Brave gates GPU features differently, so only the real browser settles it.
**On failure**: keep WASM as the default (it is), file a bug with the console output.

### The first compact recording: size, encoder cost during the call, playback

**Trigger**: compact recordings (#27) merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`; the first real Meet call recorded after that, ten minutes or longer.
**Steps**: before the call, open Settings once and check **Video format** says WebM and **Frame rate** 5 fps (a profile from before #27 is migrated on load; the row of the earlier recordings still says `.mp4`). Record the call from the toolbar button as usual. During the call, open Activity Monitor and note Brave's CPU with the recording running and, for comparison, the Brave Helper (Renderer) process of the Rescribe tab; note whether the fans come on or Meet's video stutters. After Save, look at `recording.webm` in Finder (size), then open it in Brave (drag it into a tab) and in VLC: the length must show at once, scrubbing must work, and both your voice and the call's audio must be there; look at a stretch with a shared screen and one with faces.
**Success criteria**: about 1 MB per minute of call at 5 fps (65 MB/h; an hour under 100 MB), text on the shared screen legible, faces clean; Brave's CPU with the recording running not visibly above what the call costs on its own (the AV1 encoder is software; Chromium's realtime settings held 5 fps at 1080p with headroom in the headless probe, but a real call adds Meet's own encoding); the length shown at once in Brave and VLC, seeking to the middle works.
**On failure**: the file is far above 100 MB/h: run `ffprobe -show_streams` on it and file the video stream's `codec_name` (VP9 means the AV1 fallback fired; the row's note says so) and the frame count against the duration. CPU too high or Meet stuttering: file it with the CPU figures and the machine; a lower frame rate is already in force, so the encoder is the question (Chromium's AV1 encoder is software; a VP9 or hardware H.264 default would be the fallback), and the MP4 option is the workaround meanwhile. No length or no seeking in Brave: the header patch did not apply; run `ffprobe` on the file, file the `format.duration` and the first 200 bytes (`xxd -l 200`).

### The first real MP4 plays and seeks in QuickTime

**Trigger**: the first recording made with the MP4 option (Settings → Video format → MP4). Since #27 the default is WebM, which QuickTime does not play; this check is for the option.
**Steps**: open `recording.mp4` from the export folder in QuickTime Player and in Finder's preview; scrub to the middle; check the duration shown.
**Success criteria**: plays with audio, scrubbing works, duration matches the recording. MediaRecorder writes a _fragmented_ MP4 (`moof`+`mdat` per keyframe, `mfra` at the end; see [[ENVIRONMENT_NOTES]] § "MediaRecorder cadence"), which is what this check is about.
**On failure**: remux on export (a `moov`-first, non-fragmented MP4) becomes a task; file it as a bug with the symptom.

### The toolbar path records a real Meet tab in Brave, without leaving it

**Trigger**: the vertical slice merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`; again after "stay in the call" (the toolbar click on a Meet tab no longer focuses the Rescribe tab) merged; again after the stop dialog merged (the click while recording opens a window instead of the Rescribe tab); again after the start dialog merged (the click opens a window with Start Capture, on every tab; the entry after the stop dialog's covers that window).
**Steps**: choose an export folder in the Rescribe tab, then close the Rescribe tab. Join a Meet call (a solo test call is enough), click the Rescribe toolbar button on the Meet tab, then **Start Capture** in the window that opens. Talk for 10 s, then click Stop in the Rescribe tab. Then click the toolbar button on any non-Meet tab (a YouTube tab is fine), Start Capture, and Stop again.
**Success criteria**: on the Meet tab the badge shows `REC` and **you stay on the Meet tab**: the Rescribe tab appears pinned, in the background, and takes the focus only if Brave has never granted Rescribe the microphone (the permission prompt needs a visible tab; the next recording stays in the background). You still hear the call while recording (tab capture mutes the tab unless Rescribe plays it back) and hear no echo of your own voice; the title in the list is the Meet tab's title; after Stop the list shows `recorded` with the folder path, and `<folder>/<date> <time> <title>/recording.mp4` plays with both your voice and the tab's audio. `Alt+Shift+R` does the same. On the non-Meet tab the Rescribe tab stays in the background too and records that tab.
**Channel purity step**: make a second recording where you stay silent for its whole length while the tab plays someone talking (a YouTube tab is fine). The row's levels readout must show `mic` far below `display` (at least 25 dB lower; a mic that picks up the speakers through echo cancellation sits around −50 dBFS or lower). If `mic` is within 10 dB of `display`, echo cancellation is not doing its job and me-versus-them attribution will bleed: file it with the two numbers.
**On failure**: "Couldn't capture that tab (…)" in the start window means `getMediaStreamId` failed in Brave: file a bug with the message. "Audio is blocked in this tab" means Brave's autoplay policy stopped the AudioContexts without a gesture (headless Chromium allowed it): the fix is a click in the Rescribe tab before the toolbar click, then a redesign of the start sequence. The Rescribe tab comes forward on a Meet tab with the microphone already granted: run `navigator.permissions.query({ name: 'microphone' })` in the Rescribe tab's console; `prompt` or an error means Brave's permission query does not report the grant and `micWillPrompt` in `app.ts` needs another signal. No `REC` badge and no Rescribe tab in front after a few seconds: the start is stuck in the background; click the toolbar button again, which brings the tab forward with whatever it waits on, and file what it shows. A start that is silently missing in a background tab (no note, no badge) is the same bug.

### The stop dialog in Brave: the window, its pickers, its permission prompt, and leaving a real call

**Trigger**: the stop dialog merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`. The e2e proves the whole flow in headless Chromium against OPFS stand-ins for the folders; only Brave settles the window itself, the real pickers and the real permission prompt from it, and only a real Meet call settles the call-ended signal.
**Steps**:

1. In a Meet call, click the toolbar button to start (badge `REC`), talk a few seconds, click it again. A small Rescribe window opens near the top right of the browser window showing `RECORDING`, the call's title, the elapsed time ticking and `Save to <folder>/<date> <time> <title>/`. Press Escape: it closes and the badge stays `REC`. Click the button again, then **Save**: the window says `Saved to …/` and closes; the row in the Rescribe tab reaches `done` and the folder has the four files.
2. Record again, click the button, **Save as…**: Brave's folder picker opens from the window; pick another folder. The video and, after transcription, the transcript land there; the row shows `<that folder>/<date> …/`; Settings still names the old folder.
3. Restart Brave (the folder permission goes back to `prompt`), record again, click the button, **Save**: the permission prompt appears from the window; allow it. The row must show the folder path, not `Not exported: Rescribe needs permission…`.
4. Record again, click **Discard** twice: the window says `Discarded.`, the row is gone, nothing new in the folder.
5. Record a call with someone (or a solo call), then click Meet's **Leave call**. Within about 5 s the window opens with `RECORDING` still on; press Escape and **Rejoin**: the badge is still `REC` and the recording continues (its row keeps ticking). Leave again, and this time close the Meet tab: the window shows `ENDED`, the badge says `SAVE`; Escape. Before saving it, join another call and click the toolbar button there: it records (badge `REC`), the waiting one does not stand in the way. Then find both rows in the Rescribe tab, the waiting one with **Save**, **Save as…** and **Discard**, and Save from there.
   **Success criteria**: every step as described; in step 3 the prompt shows from the window and the export succeeds without a visit to the Rescribe tab.
   **On failure**: no window on the click: Brave refused `chrome.windows.create` for a popup; the service worker's console (`brave://extensions` → service worker) has the error; file it. Step 3 ending in `Not exported: Rescribe needs permission…` means a grant given in the dialog window is not seen by the app tab's handle: the fallback is Save now on the row, and the fix is a `focus-app` before the export when the permission is `prompt`; file it. Step 5 without a window within 10 s: Meet's leave page still has elements matching `[data-participant-id]`, or the lobby preview has one; run `document.querySelectorAll('[data-participant-id]').length` in the Meet tab's console on the leave page and file the number. A window that opens mid-call while nobody left: a layout without tiles for over 5 s; file which layout (presentation, spotlight) with the same console count.
   **Since the start dialog (2026-09-25)**: in step 1 the first click opens the start window, Start Capture starts; after **Save** the stop window closes and a new tab with the recording's progress opens instead of `Saved to …/` in the window; step 4 (Discard) still answers in the window.

### The start dialog as an overlay in the page, in Brave

**Trigger**: the in-page start dialog merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions` (Brave asks nothing new: `activeTab` and `scripting` carry no install warning for an unpacked extension, but check the card for an error). The e2e proves the overlay on a strict-CSP https page with a faked click and host access; only the real click proves the `activeTab` grant covers the injection, and only Meet proves its page lets the frame in.
**Steps**:

1. On an ordinary site (a news page), click the toolbar button. A Rescribe panel appears inside the page, 12 px from its top right, the page not dimmed and still scrollable beside it; no separate window. The button again closes it; again, then Escape, closes it.
2. In a Meet call (or its lobby), click the button: the same panel over Meet's top right, with the call's title, your microphones by name, and **Start Capture**. Start: the panel goes, the badge shows `REC`.
3. Open it on `brave://extensions` (or a new tab page): the small window opens instead, as before.
4. In the panel click **Change…**: the Rescribe tab comes forward; pick a folder under Settings, go back to the page: the panel shows the new folder.
   **Success criteria**: every step as described.
   **On failure**: the window instead of the panel on an ordinary site: the injection failed; the service worker's console (`brave://extensions` → service worker) logs `no overlay on this page` with the reason; file it. A blank or refused panel on Meet: Meet's page blocked the frame; file the console error from the Meet tab.

### The start dialog and the processing tab in Brave

**Trigger**: the start dialog merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`. The e2e proves the routing, the settings and the page states in headless Chromium with fake devices; only Brave settles the windows, the real microphone names, the real picker from the start window and a real tab capture from Start Capture.
**Steps**:

1. On any tab, click the toolbar button. A small Rescribe window opens near the top right showing the tab's title, a **Microphone** selector, **Save to** with the folder from Settings and **Change…**, and **Start Capture**. Click the toolbar button again: the window closes, nothing recorded. Click it once more, press Escape: the same.
2. Open it again. The microphone list names your real devices (e.g. "Studio Display Microphone") and ends with "No microphone"; if it shows only "Default microphone", Rescribe has never been allowed the microphone in Brave: make one recording, then reopen. Pick a device, then **Change…**: Brave's folder picker opens from the window; pick a folder. The window shows it; the Rescribe tab's Settings show it too (open the pinned tab to see).
3. In a Meet call (a solo one is fine), click the button and **Start Capture**: the badge shows `REC`, you stay on the Meet tab, the window is gone. Talk 10 s, click the button, **Save**: the stop window closes and a new tab opens in front showing "Saving the video…", then the transcription phases with a progress bar (the one-time model download the first time), then `Done` with `<folder>/<date> <time> <title>/`. Close that tab mid-transcription: the row in the Rescribe tab keeps going and the folder gets the transcript files.
4. The toolbar icon shows nothing while transcribing; click it then: the start window says `Transcribing “<title>” in the background` with **Show**, which opens the progress tab again.
5. Close the Rescribe tab, click the button on a tab, **Start Capture**, and press Escape while the window still says `Starting…` (the Rescribe tab has to boot first): no `REC`, nothing recorded, the Rescribe tab reappears pinned and idle.
6. Click the button in a Meet lobby (before **Join now**), join, then **Start Capture**: it records (the capture grant survives the join). Then unplug or disable the microphone you picked in step 2 and Start again: it records with the default microphone and the row's mic source names it.
   **Success criteria**: every step as described; in step 3 the recording's row shows the microphone you picked in step 2 as its mic source.
   **On failure**: no window on the click: as for the stop dialog. The picker closing the window, or the folder not changing: file it; the window is a popup-type window, which is meant to survive a picker. Start Capture answering "Couldn't capture that tab (…)": `getMediaStreamId` refused the tab from a Start made after the click; file the message, and note whether the tab navigated between the click and Start (the grant lasts until it navigates). The progress tab opening inside the small window instead of the browser window: `chrome.windows.getLastFocused({ windowTypes: ['normal'] })` returned nothing; file it. A microphone list with the wrong device recorded: check the row's mic source label against the pick.

### The export folder permission is re-requested on Stop

**Trigger**: the first recording after restarting Brave with a folder already chosen (the handle persists, its permission may not).
**Steps**: with the Settings hint saying "Chrome will ask for permission again on the next export", record a few seconds and click Stop.
**Success criteria**: the permission prompt appears from the Stop click; after allowing, the recording exports. Deny it once: the recording is listed as `recorded` with "Not exported: …", its media is kept in OPFS, and the row's **Save now** button prompts again and exports.
**On failure**: the prompt does not appear, or appears after the export failed: `ensureExportPermission` in `onStop` is not running inside the gesture; file a bug.

### Chrome's "Stop sharing" bar ends the recording cleanly

**Trigger**: the first picker-path recording in Brave (Record screen… → a window or the entire screen).
**Steps**: record a window, then click "Stop sharing" on Brave's bar instead of Stop in the Rescribe tab. (The e2e dispatches a synthetic `ended` on the track; only this check exercises Chrome's real one.)
**Success criteria**: the Rescribe tab returns to idle and the recording is listed `recorded`. Because no click happened, the export may need the folder permission: if the row shows "Not exported" and a **Save now** button, clicking it must export. System audio from a window or screen share lands in `display.pcm` (Brave 152 on macOS 26 should offer it; the tab share always carries tab audio).
**On failure**: the status stays `recording`: the track's `ended` event did not fire; fix forward in `recorder.ts`.

### Re-export writes into the real folder in Brave

**Trigger**: the library (#4) merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`, with at least one exported recording in the list.
**Steps**: in Finder, delete `recording.mp4` from that recording's folder (or the whole folder). In the Rescribe tab, click **Re-export** on its row. Then restart Brave, open Rescribe and click **Re-export** again on the same row. Then, with a `done` recording in the list, delete its `recording.mp4` in Finder and click **Delete media for exported recordings** twice. Finally move the whole export folder elsewhere in Finder (Settings still names it) and click **Re-export** on any row.
**Success criteria**: the first click recreates the file (or the folder at the same path) and the row's status line says `Saved recording.mp4 to <path>/.`; after the restart the click prompts for the folder permission first (the persisted handle is `prompt` again) and, once allowed, writes the file. The bulk delete keeps that recording's media and names its folder in `Kept the media of 1 recording whose exported video is missing: …` (the e2e only proves this against an OPFS stand-in; the real check is that Brave's persisted handle reports the deleted file as `NotFoundError` rather than serving a stale entry). With the folder moved, the row shows `Not exported: Export folder: NotFoundError…` and nothing is deleted or created; choosing the folder again in Settings makes Re-export work. The e2e exercises the same code against an OPFS directory standing in for the folder; only Brave's File System Access implementation settles the real-disk, moved-folder and permission-prompt halves.
**On failure**: a row error `Not exported: Rescribe needs permission…` with no prompt means `ensureExportPermission` ran outside the gesture; `Not exported: Export folder: …` on an intact folder names a File System Access error to file with the message; a bulk delete that frees the media of a recording whose exported video you deleted means `exportedFileState` got a stale answer from the handle: file it with the Brave version.

### Meet speaker attribution names both people in a real two-person call

**Trigger**: the self-tile fix (self by microphone) merged and the extension reloaded at `brave://extensions`; the 58-minute call of 2026-09-25 11:30 (`2026-09-25 11-30 Meet - swu-pxhm-rzs/`) is the recording to check. Before the fix its transcript had two thirds of the remote speaker's segments labelled with the human's Meet name, because Meet named his own tile "Ulf Aslak", not "You"; the fix was replayed against that call's event log and segments (participants `Ulf, Carolina Pinheiro`, `selfTile` at 96% mic share, no display segment under the human's name). What only the video settles is below.
**Steps**: **Re-run transcription** on that row (it assembles again from the kept recogniser output, no transcription), then open `transcript.md` and `meta.json`.

1. **Self tile**: `meta.json` `selfTile.name` is your Meet display name and `micShare` is above 0.9; `participants` lists you under the name from Settings and no "(Presentation)" tiles.
2. **Names, five-minute sample**: pick any five minutes and check every turn against the video: your turns carry the name from Settings, hers carries her Meet display name; "Speaker" means no platform interval was near the segment (35 of her 304 segments before the fix, mostly short ones; note how many remain and whether they are her words).
3. **Handovers from the end of the call** (the last ten minutes): five where the new speaker opens with a short utterance ("Yes", "Right", a name) and five where the previous speaker closes with one. Check each against the video. The event log of this call showed no clock drift (DEFERRED § "Anchor platform stamps to the audio clock"), so a wrong label here is lag, not drift.
4. **Three people, once**: record a call with two other people for a few minutes, one of them mostly listening. `participants` lists all three (the listener too, from the roster the adapter now re-sends every 30 s), the listener's few words are under their name where Meet's indicator caught them and under "Speaker" where it did not, never under the other person's name (the one-remote rule must stay off).
5. **Presenting, once**: share your screen for a minute in a two-person call. `participants` is unchanged (no "(Presentation)" tile, no "You are presenting"), and her replies during the share are still under her name.
   **Success criteria**: 1 as stated; 2 has no wrong name; 3 has the short utterances under the right name in both directions; 4 and 5 as stated.
   **On failure**: `selfTile` missing or under 0.5: the mic channel's segments did not line up with the tile's speaking; file it with `meta.json` and the row's levels. Openers credited to the previous speaker mean the indicator lags more than 0.3 s: raise `MEET_LEAD` in `src/lib/evidence.ts`. Closers credited to the next speaker mean it lags less: lower it (the 11:30 call's best shift of +0.1 to +0.25 s says the lead is, if anything, slightly too large). The `makeCall` fixture in `src/lib/attribution.test.ts` puts the safe range at 0.2–0.4 s for a 200–400 ms onset lag; measure before picking a new value: `transcript.json`'s `intervals` carry the cleaned platform intervals with the lead already subtracted from each start, and `recordings/<id>/events.jsonl` in the extension's OPFS (DevTools → Application → Storage) the raw stamps.
