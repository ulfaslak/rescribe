## Runtime topology

Four execution contexts, each with a distinct job:

| Context | File | Lifetime | Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service worker | `background.ts` | ephemeral, event-driven | toolbar click and keyboard command, badge, `chrome.tabCapture.getMediaStreamId` with `consumerTabId` = the app tab (the id is bound to a render process; the app tab is cross-origin isolated and not in the service worker's process), issued last because it expires after 10 s, opens/focuses the one app tab (closes duplicates, reloads one that does not answer, badge `!` when none answers), sends `toggle-recording` to that tab only |
| App tab | `app/app.ts` | while open; pinned; warns on close when busy | owns the recorder, the audio graph, OPFS writes, the transcription pipeline (`app/pipeline.ts`), export, settings UI, library UI. `query-status` answers the recorder's state: `idle` while a transcription runs, so a toolbar click still starts a recording; the pill and the badge show the transcription then. **Single owner:** a second app tab that finds a sibling answering `query-status` renders only "already open in another tab" and never listens |
| Content script | `content/meet.ts` | per Meet page | observes the DOM, emits speaker events and roster to the extension via `chrome.runtime.sendMessage`; mirrors every message as a `rescribe:event` CustomEvent on `window` for the e2e harness (Meet's scripts cannot read the detail across worlds) |
| Storage worker | `workers/storage.ts` | per recording | appends video chunks and PCM blocks to OPFS through one sync access handle per file, flushing after every write; one queue keeps arrival order |
| Transcribe worker | `workers/transcribe.ts` | spawned on the first job, lives as long as the app tab or until a transcribe failure | VAD, then parakeet.js on WASM (default) or WebGPU; one channel per job, jobs serialised, the model kept loaded across jobs and reloaded when a job names another model or backend or after a failure |

Messages that cross `chrome.runtime` (service worker, app tab, content scripts) are typed in `src/lib/messages.ts`; the union there is the single roster of those message types. A worker's `postMessage` protocol is typed in the worker's own file and imported by its one client, so adding a worker touches no shared file.

## Data model (`src/lib/types.ts`)

- `ChannelId`: `'mic' | 'display'`. Two channels, always. A recording may have only one of them.
- `Source`: what a channel captured (`microphone`, `tab`, `window`, `screen`) plus a label (tab title, device name).
- `Recording`: id (UUID), `startedAt`/`endedAt` (epoch ms), title, sources, status (`recording → recorded → transcribing → done | failed`; `done` and `failed` re-run through `transcribing`), container, export path (set once the video is in it), `error` (why it failed; or, when it did not fail, why it is not fully exported: a `recorded` recording without an export path, a `done` one whose transcript stayed in OPFS), `note` (non-failure information: recovered after an interruption, a Meet adapter diagnostic, padded render gaps), `levels`, and at `done` the `model`, `backend` and `transcribedAt` (epoch ms) that produced the transcript.
- `Segment`: `{ channel, start, end, text, words? }`, seconds from recording start. Output of the ASR engine, per channel.
- `SpeakerInterval`: `{ channel, speaker, start, end, source }` with `source` one of `platform | model | channel`. The unit of speaker evidence. Adapters, the diarizer and channel identity all produce these.
- `LabelledSegment`: a `Segment` plus `speaker` and the `evidence` that decided it.
- `Turn`: consecutive labelled segments by the same speaker, merged for rendering.
- `Settings`: path template, my name, container, mic default, model choice, backend (`wasm` | `webgpu`), auto-transcribe (run the pipeline as soon as a recording stops; on by default).

## Clock

Recording-relative seconds everywhere in `lib/`. `startedAt` is the epoch ms of the first captured sample, defined precisely as: `Date.now()` taken in the app tab in the same task that creates both AudioWorklet taps (`Recorder.start`, after the AudioContexts are running); `performance.now()` is taken in the same instant as its monotonic twin. Both taps begin at the next render quantum, within ~10 ms. From then on each tap writes exactly one sample per 1/16000 s of *rendered* context time, zeros when its input is absent, so `sample_index / 16000` in either PCM file is recording-relative seconds from `startedAt`, with two qualifications:

- **Render gaps are zero-padded.** When the render thread does not run (an output-device change restarts the sink, a suspended context), no samples are produced and the files would fall behind the clock. The recorder compares each block's position with the monotonic time since start (`recorder/gap.ts`); a lag over 250 ms that persists for a whole second (a main-thread stall drains back to zero within its burst and never pads) is written as that many zero samples into **both** channel files before the block, and `events.jsonl` gets `{ type: 'clock-gap', at, position, paddedSamples }`. The audio between the gap and its detection (up to one second) stays early by the gap. The recording's note counts the gaps.
- **Drift is not corrected.** The audio clock and `Date.now()` disagree by their crystals' ppm; over hours that reaches the width of the attribution tolerance. [[DEFERRED]] § "Anchor platform stamps to the audio clock" is the fix.

The MediaRecorder starts in the same task; its video may begin a frame later, which is playback, not pipeline timing. Content scripts stamp with `Date.now()`; the app tab keeps the stamps as received (`events.jsonl`, written by `Recorder.appendEvent`: `app.ts` forwards every `speaker`, `roster` and `adapter-diagnostic` message that arrives while recording, and on the toolbar path only those from the recorded tab) and converts when it builds evidence (`eventsToEvidence` in `lib/evidence.ts`, through `lib/clock.ts`, called by `assembleTranscript`), so evidence can be rebuilt from disk. See [[INVARIANTS]].

## Storage

- **OPFS**, per recording directory `recordings/<id>/`: `video.mp4` or `video.webm` (MediaRecorder chunks appended), `mic.pcm` and `display.pcm` (16 kHz mono Int16 little-endian, raw, no header; each present only when that channel had an audio track; appended by the AudioWorklet taps via the storage worker), `events.jsonl` (created empty at start; `Recorder.appendEvent` appends one JSON line per adapter message received while recording, as received, and per padded render gap; `parseEventLog` in `lib/assemble.ts` reads it back and skips a malformed line). These four are opened, truncated and written only by the storage worker during the recording. After it, the pipeline writes the **artifacts** through `createWritable` from the app tab (`store.ts` `writeArtifact`): `transcript.md`, `transcript.json`, `meta.json` and `segments.<channel>.json` (the recogniser's output for one channel with the model, backend and Rescribe version that produced it; a re-run skips a channel whose file matches, so a failure on the other channel, a new "my name" or deleted media does not transcribe it again). **Exportable artifact** (`opfs.ts` `isExportableArtifact`): any file in the directory that is not media (`isMediaFile`: the video, the PCM), not `events.jsonl`, not `segments.*.json`, not a `*.crswap` swap file. **Delete media** (`deleteMedia`) removes the video and the PCM only and sets `Recording.mediaDeleted`; the event log, the artifacts and the segments cache stay, so the transcript can still be exported and, from the cache, assembled again (a re-run with another "my name"); without the cache a re-run fails. `deleteRecording` removes the directory and the record.
- **IndexedDB** `rescribe`: `recordings` (the `Recording` records, keyed by id), `handles` (the export directory handle under key `export`).
- **chrome.storage.local**: `Settings` minus the handle.
- **Export folder** (user's disk): `<template>/recording.mp4|webm` written on stop, streamed from the OPFS file; the exportable artifacts when transcription completes, copied from OPFS (`export.ts` `exportArtifacts`) into the recording's directory as it is: `findExportDirectory` resolves `exportPath` under the current export folder without creating anything and requires the exported video in it, otherwise `ExportError` `missing`. `<template>` comes from `lib/pathtemplate.ts` (`/` nests directories) and the last segment gets ` (2)`, ` (3)`… when it already exists. `Recording.exportPath` is that relative path. When the video export fails (no folder chosen; the folder's permission not granted, which is the state after a browser restart or when `finalize` ran outside a click: the share ended from Chrome's bar, or a recovery on app start) the recording stays `recorded` with `exportPath` unset and `error` set; transcription still runs and the artifacts wait in OPFS: the recording ends `done` and `error` still says why ("Not exported: …", never `done` without an export path and without that reason). When only the artifacts' export fails, the recording is `done` with `error` "Transcript not exported: …"; when its directory is no longer under the current folder (the human chose another folder, moved or deleted it), `done` with "Not exported: the recording's folder was not found under the current export folder". Either way the row shows **Save now** (`app.ts` `canSaveNow`: `recorded` without an export path, or `done` with an `error`; never while the pipeline owns the recording), which works from the stored record, re-requests the permission from the click, runs `finalize` (the video, unless it is already in its directory; it keeps a `done` recording `done`) and then `exportArtifacts`, and clears `error` only when both succeeded.
- **Storage client** (`storage/store.ts`, `storage/export.ts`), what transcription and the library call: `listRecordings`, `getRecording`, `putRecording`, `updateRecording(id, patch)`, `getVideoFile(id, container)`, `getChannelFile(id, channel)` (a `File` to slice), `readChannelPcm(id, channel)` (the whole channel as `Int16Array`) + `pcmToFloat32`, `writeArtifact(id, name, text)`, `readArtifact(id, name)`, `listArtifacts(id)` (the exportable artifacts present), `readEventLog(id)`, `segmentsArtifactName(channel)`, `deleteMedia(id)` (video and PCM only), `deleteRecording(id)`; `getExportRoot`/`setExportRoot`, `ensureExportPermission()` (call inside a user gesture), `getExportDirectory(rec, template)` (creates or reopens `<template>/`), `findExportDirectory(rec)` (the existing directory with the video, or `missing`), `writeExportFile(dir, name, data)` (Blob, bytes, string or stream), `exportArtifacts(rec)` (every exportable artifact in OPFS into that directory), `exportVideoName(container)`.

## Pipeline

All five steps are built. Steps 1–2 live in `recorder/recorder.ts`; 3–5 in `app/pipeline.ts` (`transcribeRecording(id)`, `onPipelineProgress`, `isPipelineBusy`, `isTranscribing(id)`), which runs one recording at a time and each channel on its own (the worker restarts after a transcribe failure and rejects whatever is queued behind it), and starts by itself when a recording reaches `recorded`, exported or not (Settings "Transcribe as soon as a recording stops", on by default), or from the library. `transcribeRecording` marks the record `transcribing` **when it is queued**, not when its turn comes, so the stored status is what is happening and a library action keyed on it never touches a queued recording; it emits `queued` then, `done` after the final write, and the phases in between. `app.ts` renders the list again on every phase change and exposes `window.rescribe.transcribeRecording(id)` for DevTools and the e2e harness.

1. Capture: streams → two `AudioContext`s (`recorder/graph.ts`): the 16 kHz tap context with one `AudioWorkletNode` tap per channel → 200 ms Int16 blocks → storage worker; the mix context with every channel → `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode` → `MediaRecorder` (1 s timeslice, a keyframe per timeslice) with the video track → storage worker. Tab capture also plays the display channel through the mix context's speakers, since Chrome mutes a captured tab for the human.
2. Stop: MediaRecorder stop → last chunk written → taps flushed → storage worker closes the files → `endedAt` → `finalize`: export the video into `<template>/`, mark `recorded`. A record found at `recording` on app start (tab closed, browser crashed) is finalized from what reached disk (`recoverInterrupted`), `endedAt` from the PCM length, or marked `failed` when no video reached disk.
3. Transcribe: per channel present in OPFS, `readChannelPcm` → the transcribe worker (VAD → voiced regions → Parakeet) → `Segment[]`, saved as `segments.<channel>.json`. Progress (model download per file, load, regions) reaches `onPipelineProgress` listeners, the Record section's status line while nothing is being recorded, and the badge (`processing`). A failure marks the recording `failed` with the channel and the stage; a re-run reuses the channels that succeeded. No channel file and no cached channel: `done` with an empty transcript when the video is there (a video-only recording), `failed` when it is not (the media was deleted), the earlier failure's reason kept in front.
4. Attribute: the record is read again first (its title, export path and notes may have moved while the channels ran). `assembleTranscript` (`lib/assemble.ts`, pure): `eventsToEvidence` over the speaker and roster lines of `events.jsonl` with `selfLabel` = Settings "my name", plus channel identity spanning the recording (mic = "my name", display = "Speaker") → `attribute()` → `LabelledSegment[]`.
5. Render: `toTurns()` → `transcript.md`; `transcript.json` (turns, labelled segments with words, the model, the backend, the evidence intervals); `meta.json` (`{ version, id, title, startedAt, endedAt, sources, participants, model, backend, container, exportPath, levels, clockGaps, transcribedAt, rescribeVersion }`). Written to OPFS first, then exported into the video's directory (§ Storage: `exportArtifacts`, never a directory without the video); mark `done`, with `error` when anything is not in the folder.

A recording found at `transcribing` on app start was interrupted (reload, crash, while queued or running): `recoverInterruptedTranscriptions` puts it back to `recorded` with a note and the app runs it again once, whatever the auto-transcribe setting; a recording recovered from `recording` follows the setting. Each stage is a function over data on disk, so any stage can be re-run from the library: `transcribeRecording` on a `done` or `failed` recording runs 3–5 again with the cached channels; a `failed` recording's error moves to its note ("Earlier failure: …") so the reason survives the re-run.
